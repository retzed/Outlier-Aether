Task Overview

Welcome to the Live S2S Elo Project

In this project, you will evaluate two voice AI assistants by completing blind, side-by-side voice
conversations based on an assigned scenario. Your goal is to compare how each model performs
while keeping both conversations as consistent as possible in terms of context, effort, and user
intent.

Before beginning each conversation, review the assigned Scenario, What To Do, and Skills Tested
sections. Throughout the task, engage naturally with both models, maintain the same overall
conversational objective, and allow each conversation to develop organically based on the model's
responses.

---

## Page 7

After completing both conversations, you will evaluate the models across multiple dimensions,
including Overall Preference, Naturalness & Engagement, Conversational Dynamics, Utility, Audio
Quality, Task Success, and Error Clusters. Your ratings should be supported by clear,
evidence-based rationales that reference specific moments from each conversation.

These guidelines are designed to serve as a step-by-step reference while you are tasking. Keep this
document available throughout your work to ensure your conversations, evaluations, and
justifications remain consistent with project expectations.

DO’s & DON’Ts

QUICK START! ​

These DOs and DON’Ts serve as a quick-start guide to our project’s goals, helping you avoid

common errors and deliver the highest-quality submissions possible!

➔​ Do review the Scenario before you begin. Read the Scenario, What To Do, and

Skills Tested sections so you understand the objective before starting the

conversation.

➔​ Do maintain Scenario Adherence. Follow the assigned scenario and stay focused

on the requested objective throughout the conversation.

➔​ Do maintain Scenario Coherence. Give both Model A and Model B the same

context, level of detail, effort, and opportunity to succeed. Keep both

conversations as similar as possible while allowing them to develop naturally.

➔​ Do have a natural multi-turn conversation. Engage in a genuine back-and-forth

that gives both models enough opportunities to demonstrate their conversational

abilities.

➔​ Do respond naturally. Answer the model's follow-up questions, acknowledge its

➔​ Do embrace the assigned role or persona. If the scenario requires roleplay, stay in

follow-up questions, corrections, or interruptions to observe how well each model

➔​ Do evaluate the complete conversation. Consider the overall interaction rather

2.2.1 Type of Scenarios: IQ vs EQ vs CD Scenario Guide – Overview

There are different types of scenarios, each designed to test different kinds of
intelligence:

➔​ EQ Scenarios test emotional intelligence (empathy, tone, social awareness,

creative collaboration, companionship). The experience is the product.

➔​ IQ Scenarios test informational intelligence (accuracy, reasoning, structure,

factual delivery). The information or result is the product.

➔​ Hybrid Scenarios test both elements of EQ and IQ Scenarios.

➔​ CD Scenarios test knowing when to speak, listen, yield, or stop.

Depending on the type of scenario, a conversation may need to unfold and be
evaluated differently. Each type of scenario requires the model:

➔​ To reason in different ways.

➔​ Adapt to the context.

➔​ Prioritize different capabilities in its response.

➔​ Determine which dimensions should carry greater weight in the evaluation.

🧠Main Principle
The dimensions remain the same; only their priority changes. When in doubt, ask:
“What was the user trying to get out of this interaction?”.

➔​ The plan should guide the conversation, not become a pre-written script. Your

2.4 Conversation Expectations

Your conversations should meet the following expectations:

Scenario Adherence​

Follow the assigned scenario, objective, and constraints throughout both

conversations. Do not replace the assigned topic with a different one or leave out

required elements.

Scenario Coherence​

Maintain equivalent starting conditions, context, effort, and conversational depth for

both models. Minor differences are expected because each model may respond

differently.

Natural Interaction​

Respond in real time to each model. Acknowledge its answers, respond to relevant

follow-up questions, and allow the conversation to develop organically.

Multi-Turn Engagement​

Have enough back-and-forth interaction to evaluate how the model performs over

time. One exchange is not sufficient.

Persona Adherence​

When the scenario requires a specific role, tone, character, or emotional state, act it

out consistently and give the model a fair opportunity to respond to it.

Fair Comparison​

Both models should receive comparable attention and effort. Keep the number of turns

and overall depth as close as reasonably possible.

Refer to the Audit Criteria: How your submissions will be graded appendix for

an in-depth description and common errors to avoid.

2.5 North Star

We want a chatbot that talks like a smart, charismatic friend — natural, useful,

accurate, and easy to follow. Not a system reading out a canned answer.

The ideal model should sound like a smart, charismatic friend who is:

➔​ Natural and engaging

➔​ Useful and relevant

➔​ Reasonably accurate

➔​ Easy to follow

➔​ Responsive to the user’s tone and intent

➔​ Consistent with any requested persona

Default rule: When models are otherwise comparable, naturalness and engagement

serve as the primary differentiators—but only once accuracy is reasonable and

acceptable (i.e., no clearly made-up information or factual errors that obviously impact

the prompt’s goals).

⚠️ Important Note

➔​ Do not force both conversations to follow the exact same wording after the

opening prompt. Keep the goals and key information equivalent, but adapt

naturally to each model’s responses.

2.6 Voice Skin and Persona / System Instruction — Only for Skin Voice Tasks

Listen and read the Voice Skin clip and the Persona/System Instruction, respectively,

and make sure you understand the difference:

The Persona (text system instruction) provides the personality to our voice model

similar to how a voice actor can embody multiple characters when receiving

instructions/notes from a voice director.

●​ The system instruction is the yardstick for persona adherence.

Score how faithfully and naturally each model behaves like this persona. Check Step

4. Rate Conversation — Skin Voice UI

The model Voice Skin is a voice "costume" similar to the base voice of a voice actor.

Think of the perceived age/gender presentation, timbre , and accent/locale (e.g. its the

voice actor audition/casting call).

●​ The same skin can provide, with the same timbral characteristics, a diverse

number of personalities / characters (which are defined by the system

instruction: helpful assistant, chatty friend, etc).

●​ Do not judge only persona adherence on how closely the delivery matches

the voice skin’s implied vibe.

Step 3: Speak with the Models

3.1 Complete the Conversations

When you're ready to begin, select Tap to Start Talking and begin speaking naturally

with the model.

You will complete two separate conversations:

➔​ Model A

➔​ Model B

Each conversation should follow the assigned scenario while allowing the interaction to

develop naturally. Both models should receive comparable context, effort, and

conversational depth so they can be fairly evaluated.

When you have gathered enough information to evaluate the model, end the

conversation and repeat the same process with the second model.

3.2 Scenario Adherence

Throughout both conversations, follow the assigned scenario as closely as possible.

Maintain the same:

➔​ Scenario

➔​ Objective

➔​ Context

➔​ User intent

➔​ Required constraints

➔​ Overall conversational goals

Minor differences in wording are expected because each conversation should feel

natural, but both models should receive equivalent information.

✅ Good Scenario Adherence Example

Model A

"Can you recommend an apple pie recipe?"

Model B

"Could you suggest a recipe for apple pie?"

Explanation

💡 Important Note

Use the scenario to guide the conversation, but introduce it naturally in your own

words. Never read the scenario instructions verbatim or provide one model with

information that the other model does not receive.

3.3 Natural Conversation

The goal is to evaluate how the models perform during a realistic conversation.

As the interaction develops:

➔​ Respond naturally to each model's replies.

➔​ Answer relevant follow-up questions.

➔​ Allow each conversation to evolve based on what the model says.

➔​ Stay within the assigned scenario while adapting to each model's responses.

Avoid treating the conversation like a checklist or interview. Your responses should feel

spontaneous rather than rehearsed.

⚠️ Important Note

Do not follow a pre-written script. The opening prompt may be planned, but the

remainder of the conversation should develop naturally based on each model's

responses.

3.4 Maintaining a Fair Comparison

Both conversations should provide each model with an equal opportunity to succeed.

When comparing Model A and Model B:

➔​ Begin with equivalent prompts.

➔​ Maintain similar conversational depth.

➔​ Give both models the same level of effort and engagement.

➔​ Ask equivalent follow-up questions whenever possible.

➔​ Allow reasonable differences that naturally result from each model's responses.

Small conversational differences are expected, but the overall scenario and objective

should remain consistent.

---

## Page 17

If one model misunderstands the scenario or performs poorly, continue interacting

naturally. Do not compensate by giving that model additional hints or information

that the other model did not receive.

3.5 Adaptability

Some scenarios naturally create opportunities to test how well a model adapts during a

conversation.

When appropriate, you may:

➔​ Clarify your request.

➔​ Change your mind.

➔​ Introduce a new constraint.

➔​ Correct the model, except for Code Switch scenarios.

➔​ Ask a relevant follow-up question.

Observe whether the model:

➔​ Acknowledges the new information.

➔​ Adjusts its response appropriately.

➔​ Maintains conversational flow.

➔​ Continues responding naturally.

Adaptability should feel like part of a real conversation rather than an intentional stress

test.

3.6 When to Skip

If you are unable to complete the task because of a technical issue, select Skip

(Technical Issue).

Choose the option that best describes the issue:

➔​ The model freezes during the conversation.

➔​ The model does not respond.

➔​ Unable to connect to the model.

➔​ Other technical issues.

➔​ Unsafe or harmful model response.

When skipping a task, provide a detailed description of the issue, including any relevant

context that may help identify or reproduce the problem.

3.5.1 Other Skips

➔​ Skip the task if the model is generating Multiple Responses in one single turn.

Example: In Turn 6 the model provided three different answers, this task should be
skipped.

4.6.1 Emotional Scenarios (EQ) Hierarchy & Breakdown

NATURALNESS/ENGAGEMENT > UTILITY > AUDIO QUALITY

Naturalness is load-bearing — delivery carries the emotional register (warmth, dynamic

tone, prosody matched to the moment, neither flat nor exaggerated). If the emotional

atmosphere changes, the model should follow suit with a graceful shift, not an awkward

"gear change." Reward originality over just mirroring the user back.

●​ Was there originality, rather than just mirroring the user back?

●​ As a user, which model would you recommend to a friend who had a similar

question?

4.7 Task Success

Does the model fulfill every layer of the user's request — explicit asks, implicit

expectations, and unstated emotional needs — without adding unrequested actions or

misreading intent? Did it do exactly what was needed on this turn, including matching the

correct emotional register?

-​
Example: a user venting about their day needs validation, not a five-step action

plan. Offering unsolicited advice, here, task is a failure, even if the advice is good.

Selection options per model: Pass or Partial or Fail

Unlike Utility (a usability threshold), Task Success is the granular check: did the model

fulfill every action and nested action - explicit and implicit - including the correct

emotional register?

⚠️ Important Note

Partial should be reserved for instances in which parts of the request are clearly fulfilled

but other parts are ignored or neglected.

4.7.1 Task Success Scenario-Based Breakdown

In general, it is a Pass/fail against the scenario prompt. Mark failure if the user's goal is

not met end-to-end (even after reasonable back-and-forth), or if a catastrophic error

occurs at any point (severe safety/policy issue, clearly harmful instruction, major privacy

leak, or an irreparably wrong action) — even if the model later recovers.

How to assess Task Success based on the given scenario category

●​ Emotional (EQ) scenarios

○​ Includes hitting the emotional register: a significant delivery failure (cold

tone in emotional support, breaking character, a missed vocal adjustment)

can fail the task even when the content is correct. Giving unsolicited advice

when the user only wants an active listener is a red flag.

●​ Informational (IQ) scenarios

---

## Page 26

○​ The user got accurate, complete information that resolved the ask —

including implicit sub-asks (e.g., a five-day forecast and a flag on any

●​ Hybrid/Structural scenarios

○​ Fulfill every mode it spans, not just the strongest. Failing any load-bearing

○​ Hinges on the turn-taking itself: dominating the user, failing to yield when
